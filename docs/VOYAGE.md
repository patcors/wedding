# The Voyage (Garden story, proof of concept)

The Garden's middle chapter becomes a voyage down the river that tells Patrick and Amelia's story, replacing the photo album ("A lifetime of little moments"). The opening (their names) and the ending ("Meet us in the garden") stay. Status: proof of concept on branch `voyage-poc`. Nothing here ships until Amelia says so.

## The story in scenery

Two small sailboats travel downstream as the guest scrolls. Words are minimal: the only running text is the month and year, shown where the photo album used to be. Everything else is told by scenery.

- **Amelia's boat:** a toy sloop (`BOAT_LOOK` in `boatLooks.ts`): a glossy soft teal hull over a white bottom, a little cream cabin, and a white main and jib striped in teal, the jib eased the other way to the main, wing on wing.
- **Patrick's boat:** the same toy sloop in soft yellow. (Other identifying features, not letters, come later.)
- **The opening:** under the title each boat is tied up at its own little jetty on the outer bank of its channel, Amelia's on the left and Patrick's on the right, lanterns still burning from the night. As the guest starts to scroll they let go their lines, ease their sails and pull out into their channels.
  - **The names fly to their boats:** as the guest starts to scroll, "Amelia" glides out of the title to just above her teal boat on the left and "Patrick" to just above his yellow one on the right, so the title's order doesn't leave him on her side. On desktop, where the title is one line, they cross: Amelia's lifts over and Patrick's dips under before either sets off sideways, so they pass well clear of each other; on a phone, where the title is stacked, Patrick's rises as it goes right and Amelia's drops as it goes left. Each shrinks as it goes, landing at about 27 to 46px (3% of the screen's width), legible but small enough that the two never meet over a phone's close boats (if they would, each moves out from between them), and sits a little over its masthead, clear of the 2017 signpost just behind Amelia's mast. Each holds above its boat a moment, following it as it pulls out, then fades there; the "&" and "the journey so far" simply fade where they are as the names lift off. It is all done by .046, before the voyage's date shows at .05 and long before the meeting's stop, and goes by the eased progress the scene shows, so scrolling back brings the names home exactly. Any butterfly perched on the title lets go as the names start to move, and none lands on them until they are home. Before the scene is up, if WebGL fails, or under reduced motion, the title fades as a whole as it always did. (`openingNames.ts`, tested in `tests/openingNames.test.mjs`: `NAME_FLIGHT`'s spans of progress, where each lands and how it bows; `mastheadAt` in `storyBoats.ts`; `NameFlight.tsx` projects the mastheads through the scene's camera each frame and moves the names with CSS variables on the title's panel, never React state, so it works at any screen size.)
- **Before they meet (before July 2017):** the river is split by a small island and thick reeds. Each boat is in its own channel and can't see the other.
- **July 2017, they meet:** the channels rejoin and the boats travel side by side from then on.
- **February 2021 – March 2023 (Amelia in Sydney):** the river widens and the boats drift further apart, still in sight. They part at the nose of a low sandstone rock in the middle of the river, Amelia's on the left and Patrick's on the right, and as February 2021 shows Amelia's sails under a miniature Sydney Harbour Bridge (see "Sydney"). Sydney carries on down the river: across from 2022's pile a miniature Opera House stands on a little sandstone point on her bank. They stay apart through Amelia's two changes of work and come back alongside in March 2023.
- **The flags fly at the sterns:** each boat's university and work flags fly from two small ensign staffs at its stern, one on each quarter, low and in front of the sails as the camera sees them from behind, so the masthead is left bare until the big mast flies their monogram. The work flag flies from the outboard staff (Amelia's to the left, Patrick's to the right) and the university's from the inboard one, towards the other boat, so when both fly they read side by side, each on its own staff. A staff stands only while it has a flag: it is put up just before its first flag goes up and taken down once its last comes down, so the sterns are bare under the title and at the dock.
- **The university's flag:** while they're at university, from the meeting to graduation, both boats fly the University of Wollongong's flag, its shield on paper, from the inboard staff. It is run up as July 2017 shows, flies beside FinoComp's from November 2018, and comes down as August 2020 shows, just before the mortarboards go up.
- **Company flags:** each boat flies a paper flag from its outboard staff with the mark of whoever its sailor works for, and nothing before their internship. Both run up FinoComp's (its orange mark) as November 2018 shows and keep them into Sydney. As August 2022 shows Amelia strikes hers and runs up a plain interim flag for her months tutoring and selling haircare online; as January 2023 shows she runs up HeirWealth's (its purple crown). Patrick keeps FinoComp's until March 2023, so for a while hers is HeirWealth's and his FinoComp's. Each of these is a milestone with time of its own, so the interim flag, and then the two different flags, each fly for most of a screen (about 87svh of scroll each); each change is done as the date settles.
- **August 2024, they start dating:** while the date holds the boats come together and the first two crossbeams go across between them, and butterflies fly out of the trees on Patrick's bank to circle them (see "The dating butterflies"), and his bird has just flown home across the river, as July showed (see "The apartments"). Over the following months the hulls grow long and slim and deck planks are laid one by one between them, until just before their engagement they are one catamaran: two hulls and a shared deck.
- **January 2026, salsa classes:** as the date shows, the catamaran turns once on the spot like a dancer, everything aboard going round with it, and sails on.
- **April 2026, they get engaged:** the two small masts give way to one. Each boat furls its sail and brings its HeirWealth flag down, its stern staff is taken down, and its mast is lowered aft along its hull. While the date holds, one tall mast rises from the middle of the deck; its sail unrolls, the toy sloops' white cloth with each of their two stripes now a band of Amelia's teal under one of Patrick's yellow, and a paper flag with their P&A monogram is run up to its masthead, flying to starboard. On the deck a little man in Patrick's yellow goes down on one knee to a little woman in Amelia's teal and holds up a ring, then stands, and they ride on hand in hand (see "The proposal"). Rose petals trail behind it on the water from the engagement, a few fluttering down from the new sail as it unrolls, and ease off as it enters the bend. The catamaran sails on round the bend and docks under the one sail.
- **The wedding (April 2027):** the river makes an S-bend. Coming round it reveals a massive old tree (the old tree at Jasper's Berry) with a small jetty beneath it. The catamaran docks there.
  - **The dock turns towards the old tree (October 2026):** the dock's final view looks a little further right than it first did, so more of the old tree shows: on desktop more of its trunk and spreading crown over the festoons, and on a phone its whole trunk, right of the copy's button, where before only its edge showed at the screen's side. It is just the dock's framing (`DOCK_VIEW`'s `look`, how far from the mooring towards the tree the camera looks: .3 to .42 on desktop, .12 to .3 in portrait), so the glide settles straight onto it and reduced motion's dock still is the same picture; nothing moves once the catamaran is moored. Amelia asked for this in place of the finale's rise and pull-back (October 2026), which rose 13 units over the bend to a three-quarter view down on the dock about a second after mooring and settled back; that flight, its timing (`FINALE`), its aerial view and the haze it pushed back are gone. A slow pan once moored was considered and left out: the dock chapter has under a second of scroll after mooring, so it would have had to be timed rather than scrolled, as the rise was, and a still final framing is simpler and the same for every guest.
  - **The guests' own boats (October 2026):** on a Personal link each guest in the Party gets a little paper boat with their first name on a card sail (`guests` in `parties.ts`, so Dean and Kelly are a boat each on `/curry/`, and Kirsten, Michael and Kai three on `/crash/`), and they come in to the dock with the catamaran, so every guest finishes the story arriving at the wedding too. Amelia asked to see them (October 2026: they used to drift down the jetty's bank behind the headland's trees, so a guest only ever saw one already moored). Their arrival is a little scene of its own, played in real time (Amelia and the integrator, October 2026: at first it was tied to the scroll and lasted under a second at the story's pace, too quick to see). As the wedding's date is about to show (`GUEST_ARRIVAL.cue`, p .895), with the catamaran coming round the bend's last curve, they come out of the haze far down the river beyond the dock, in file, their names facing ahead, and sail up the river towards the catamaran for about three seconds (`up`): on desktop up its far side to wait wide of where the catamaran comes in, below the jetty, at the left of the dock's picture; on a phone, whose narrow picture looks straight down the river past the catamaran's sail, up the far side and across below where the catamaran will lie, to wait beside the jetty under the old tree, between the jetty's lamp and the tree. Once the catamaran is tied up (`guestRound`'s `gate`, p .97) they pass it on its far side, come back across the river just above its stern and drift down into their berths beside it, bow downstream as the catamaran lies, the cards turning to the camera as they settle (`across`, about four seconds; each boat `lag` behind the one before): six to seven seconds in all for a Party of two or three. Their clock (`GuestBoat.tsx`) runs in real time but never past what the scroll allows (`guestArrivalCap`: up the river in step with the catamaran's approach, round it only once it is moored, all in by p 1), so a guest who scrolls fast or sails on still sees the whole scene and finds them coming in for a few seconds after the catamaran moors, and one who scrolls slowly keeps them in step with it, waiting below the jetty until it is tied up. Scrolling back takes them back down the river with it, and back before the cue they are gone. A jump (the monogram's "Back to the beginning", say) finds them as far in as the scroll allows. They lie abreast just upstream of the T-head, between the bank and the moored catamaran's stern, a little staggered, every other card raised on a tall toothpick mast, so in the dock's picture, where they lie nearly in line with the camera, each name stands clear of the next, and the names read left to right in the Party's order. On a phone the river is narrower, so a pair or three take smaller hulls (`GUEST_SCALE.fleet`: .8 for two, .7 for three, .66 for four), and one noses in below the T-head's end, bow downstream, as it came round the head. Every card stays full size whatever the Party: one name on a place card (Cormorant Garamond in the Garden's ink on paper with a fine ruled border), the card as wide as its name needs (`guestCard`), so every guest's name in `parties.ts` reads at 12px to the em or more at the dock on a 390 × 844 phone and on a phone on its side. On a short landscape screen the cards grow as the height shrinks (`GUEST_SCALE.short`, from 600px; 1.23 of desktop's size at 844 × 390) but the hulls keep desktop's size, so they don't crowd the catamaran. They bob each a little out of step with the others. A Party of up to four is laid out (none has more than three yet; a fifth guest would get no boat). The plain Garden shows no boats. A guest who has opened their Personal link on this device sees their boats on the plain page too (`pa_party`, as the Details page reads it). Reduced motion shows them moored in the dock's still (and, at the wedding's own still, just setting off far down the river), with no motion; *Pause motion* holds their arrival and their bobbing. "Float a boat" boats bump off them once the catamaran is moored, as they do off the catamaran.
- **Dawn to dusk:** the whole journey runs through one clear day, and the light never stands still. The opening is at soft dawn, warm low on the horizon under a pale blue sky; by the time the boats meet it is a bright, light-blue morning, clear blue at midday, a deeper blue through the afternoon, golden round the bend and dusk at the dock. The sun climbs and sinks and swings across the sky the whole way, so the shadows turn as they lengthen, and a light morning haze burns off by midday and gathers again at dusk.

### Adding an egg

The contract is the comment at the top of `eggs/egg.ts`; in short:

- **Register:** add its id to `EggId` and set `egg` on its milestone in `journey.ts`, and map the id to a component in `eggs/index.tsx`. Each egg gets `EggProps`: its `milestone` and the `SlotProps`.
- **Time:** `eggTime(milestone, progress, reduced, settled)` is scroll since the date first showed, in milestone shares (`scrolledSince`): negative before, running through the date's hold. Arrive at 0; linger as long as the story allows, and leave before the dock.
- **Place:** the trees close over the river, so the only open sky is up its corridor, and portrait sees only about 17° either side of the middle. For something in the air, `inView(p, width, { distance, bearing, height, from, follow })` gives a spot relative to the camera (degrees right of the middle; `follow` below 1 lets it lag the camera's turn, as distant things do), `overRiver` keeps it over the water, clear of the trees, and `heightAt(distance, elevation)` turns degrees above the eye line into a height. Place it nearer than it looks and scale it down rather than truly far, where the canopies would hide it. Keep clear of the date label at the top centre; through the bend the only open sky is just left of it (see the balloons). Something on a bank can use a fixed world spot from `riverCenter`/`bankEdge` at `routeAtMonth(month)`.
- **Motion:** hold idle animation while `paused` (set for reduced motion too). Under `reduced` the camera cuts to each milestone's still, where `eggTime` returns `settled`: show the egg at rest there.
- **Budget:** one to three draw calls and well under 1,500 triangles, built once and disposed, no shadows, and `visible = false` outside its window. For comparison the scene draws about 2.7 to 4.05 million triangles a frame on desktop (1440 × 900) and 1.4 to 2.2 million in portrait (390 × 844), in 205 to 305 draw calls (counting the shadow and water-reflection passes; measured at every milestone's stop, every year's turn and the eggs between them in October 2026, after the 2400svh rebalance: the busiest are the meeting and dating's stop, 304 on desktop, and the engagement's petals, 4.05 million triangles).
- **Light:** the sky changes from dawn to dusk, and the haze with it (`scene.fog`'s colour and start). Read colours from `scene.fog` each frame rather than fixing them, as the balloons do for their haze.
- **Size:** on a real phone the things at the side of the river were too small to spot (Amelia, October 2026), so the eggs and year markers are drawn larger than first placed. Each keeps its own approved `scale` (the numbers in this document) and is drawn at it times a growth, `eggSize(scale, mobile, grow?)` in `eggs/egg.ts`: `EGG_GROWTH` is 1.2 on desktop and 1.5 on a phone, whose wider lens draws everything smaller, and an egg that reads better grown less passes its own `grow`. As drawn now, phone and desktop: the π, the Möbius strip, the mortarboards, the flower bed, the aerial silk and the lyra ×1.5 and ×1.2; the park bench ×1.15 and ×1.12 (any larger and it meets a trunk or leaves a phone's picture early); the candlesticks ×1.25 on a phone and as they were on desktop (larger, they stand in front of the statistics carved above them); the rubber ducks ×1.2 and ×1.1, the dating butterflies and the engagement's petals ×1.5 and ×1.2; both snow trips, flurry, patch and snowmen, ×1.15 and ×1.1 (the 2025 pair from a smaller `scale`, see "The second snow trip") (`SNOW_GROW` in `flurry.ts`: they already stood large by the water, and any larger the 2019 snowman's head would hide 2020's carving); the year markers ×1.4 and ×1.15 (`MARKER_GROW` in `yearMarkerSpots.ts`), but 2017's signpost ×1.15 on a phone (beside the carved sloop in the picture any larger), 2019's buoy ×1.3 on a phone, standing a little further out (`share` .87 there, clear of Patrick's hull), 2022's pile ×1.2 on a phone (clear of the two sloops carved behind it), and 2025's oar ×1.15 on a phone. - **`journey.ts`** is the single source of truth, pure and tested (`tests/journey.test.mjs`): `MILESTONES` (`id`, `month`, `description` for screen readers, optional `egg`, `room` and `hold`), `DATES`, when Amelia left FinoComp (`AMELIA_LEFT_FINOCOMP`, August 2022) and when she moved into Patrick's (`AMELIA_MOVED_IN`, March 2026, which nothing is timed by: the birdhouses show it early, `MOVE_SHOWN`), and the chapters over scroll progress p ∈ [0, 1]: arrival `[0, .05)`, voyage `[.05, .92)`, dock `[.92, 1]` (`ARRIVAL_END`, `VOYAGE_END`, `CHAPTERS`, `chapterAt`). The boats are moored from `DOCKED` (.97).
  - `storyMonth(p)`: story time in months since January 2017, piecewise-linear. Scroll is shared out in shares (`ROOM` of progress, about .041, 98svh of scroll). Every year from 2018 to 2023 gets the same scroll, `YEAR` (1.5 shares, 148svh), so the Januaries, and with them the year markers, come evenly however the milestones fall (Amelia's rule). Inside a year each milestone takes its `room` at its date (by default `HOLD`, .35 of a share) and the rest of the year is spread evenly over its twelve months, so a busy year's months rush and a quiet year's roll by. ## Tools

### Screenshots

`corepack pnpm shots` (`scripts/voyage-shots.mjs`, its pure parts in `scripts/voyage-shots-lib.mjs`, tested in `tests/voyageShots.test.mjs`) photographs the voyage at every milestone stop in headless Chrome, with a contact sheet and a visual diff, so nobody needs a throwaway screenshot script. `--help` lists every option.

- **What it shoots:** the opening, each milestone's stop and the dock: `STOPS` in `journey.ts`, where the arrow keys land, halfway through each date's hold. Desktop 1440 × 900 and phone 390 × 844, the phone emulated as one (mobile layout, touch, no hover). `--only meet,engaged,dock` picks stops by id, `--at .3,.55` adds any scroll progress (on its own, only those), and `--viewports` takes `desktop`, `phone`, `phone-small` (320 × 568), `phone-mid` (375 × 667), `phone-landscape` (844 × 390), `WxH` or `WxH-touch`. The stops always come from the checkout running the command, whichever server it shoots.
- **Stills by default.** It emulates reduced motion, so each stop is the scene's still for that milestone, with no butterflies or animation, and waits for downloads, fonts and the page's fades (the cover) before each shot, and counts the scroll helper as already shown so it never comes up over a still. Two runs of the same code match to within 1 level of 255 in a few dozen pixels. `--live` scrolls as a guest does instead, waits as long as the story's pace takes to get there (`paceToward` in `journeyPace.ts`) and 2.5s more for the scene to settle; it is slower and never quite the same twice.
- **Where:** `--serve` (the default) starts this checkout's dev server on a free port from 4800 (or `--port`), never 4321, 4341 or 4342, and stops it afterwards. `--url http://127.0.0.1:4351` shoots a server already running. The dev Review panel is tucked away unless `--review`. Astro 7 sends `astro dev` to the background when it detects an AI agent, so `--serve` starts it with `ASTRO_DEV_BACKGROUND=1` and `--ignore-lock` to keep it a child with its own PID (and alongside any `pnpm dev` of the same checkout).
- **Output:** `.shots/<branch>-<date>-<time>/` in the checkout (git-ignored, and not watched by the dev server; `--out` to choose): a PNG per stop and viewport (`desktop/05-graduation.png`), a contact sheet per viewport with the date and stop id under each frame (`contact-phone.png`), `manifest.json`, and `index.html`, which flips through the frames: ← → step stops, ↑ ↓ change viewport, B shows after, before and the difference.
- **Comparing:** `--compare <dir>` diffs against an earlier run. `--compare-url http://127.0.0.1:4342` shoots the same stops from another server as "before" (into `before/`), so a branch can be checked against a review server in one go. Each frame gets a before | after | difference image in `compare/`, the changed pixels in magenta over a faded copy, and the summary, printed, in `compare/summary.txt` and at the top of the index, lists the frames that changed, most first. A pixel counts as changed when a colour channel moves more than 8 levels, and a frame when more than .01% of its pixels do (`THRESHOLDS`); live, 48 levels and 2.5%, since there the water's shimmer and the trees' sway move 1 to 4% of pixels by more than 24 levels between two runs of the same code. `--threshold` and `--min-changed` override them. If the dev server swaps in changed code part way through (an edit while it runs), it notices and shoots that viewport again.
- **Clean-up:** Chrome gets a profile of its own in the system's temp folder, deleted afterwards. Chrome and the dev server each start in a process group of their own and are stopped by their PIDs, whether the run finishes, fails or is stopped with Ctrl-C. `--timeout` (90s) bounds the wait for the scene to be ready. Chrome is `/usr/bin/chromium` or the usual macOS places, or `--chrome` (or `CHROME=`); on Linux it draws with the GPU through Vulkan (SwiftShader takes minutes a frame), and `CHROME_FLAGS` adds flags.
- **Time:** a full run (18 stops, desktop and phone) takes about 35s against a running server and 50s with `--serve`, whose first load compiles the page, plus about 6s to compare; `--compare-url` shoots twice, about 70s in all. Other work on the GPU can slow it. `--live` takes about four and a half minutes for every stop on both, most of it the story's own pace from stop to stop, so it is worth narrowing with `--only`.
- Repeatable stills: in October 2026 the tool's author saw, about one run in four on the first run after a source edit, a frame differ from the same code's (trees, grass and sails at another point in their sway, or the view shifted a little). Looked into the same night: every clock in the scene already held under reduced motion (see Reduced motion), and none of some twenty runs differed beyond 1 level of 255 in a handful of pixels: three `--serve` runs, six each straight after an edit to a different scene file, and, after the fix below, five with `--compare` against the first, one beside a `--live` run loading the GPU and two with an edit saved part way through. What the tool did miss was code swapped in while the page loaded (an edit saved just before or as the run starts): it only watched for that once the scene was up, so such a page, part old code and part new with parts of the scene remounted, was shot. It now loads that viewport again. If a lone frame still changes when you didn't expect it, run again and say so in docs.

### Timeline

**Timeline (dev only).** `/dev/timeline` charts the whole voyage on one page, so spacing and crowding can be directed at a glance. Its x axis is scroll progress p from 0 to 1, with the chapters, the sky's keyframes as a colour strip (`skyCycle.ts`), each January and each milestone (a diamond where its date first shows, a ring at its stop). Under it every scenery piece has a row: the eggs, ornaments, carvings, year markers, what happens on the boats (the flags, the joining, the salsa turn, the change of masts, the proposal, the petals, the butterflies) and the river's landforms. A solid bar is when the piece is really in the picture and competing for attention, coloured by bank (Amelia's left teal, Patrick's right yellow, the river or both grey); a pale bar is in the picture but not counted (a flag simply flying, the monogram, the landforms); an outline is fading in or out, or mostly lost in the fog; a thin line is drawn but out of the picture. "In the picture" is worked out, not read off the timings: p is sampled every .0005, the camera is put where `cameraRig.ts` puts it on desktop (1440 × 900) and on a phone (390 × 844), each piece's bounding sphere (from its own geometry where it can be built in Node) is projected into the frustum, and it counts where at least half the sphere is in the frame, it is at least two pixels across, the fog (GardenSky's) hides less than half of it and its own show/hide function has it at least half faded in. Trees and the headland aren't modelled, so a piece hidden in leaves still counts. Real-time pieces (tosses, flights, the salsa turn) are drawn where they come to rest and counted from their cue to their own `late`. The lane at the foot counts the competing pieces in view at each p and shades where more than two are (`CROWDED`). Hovering a row shows its name, its windows in dates and p, and its file; hovering the lane lists what is in view; clicking anywhere offers the Garden at that moment, `/?at=<p>` (the `?at=` parameter comes from the `voyage-c-inspect` branch); the toggle switches desktop and phone, and Zoom stretches the axis. The same numbers as text, for checking crowding without a browser: `node scripts/voyage-timeline.mjs` lists the busiest moments and the crowded stretches on both layouts (`--layout phone`, `--pieces` for every piece's windows, `--at 0.62` for what is in view at a moment, `--json`). The pieces come from `sceneryCatalog.ts`, built from the scene's own exported spots, show/hide functions and geometry; the few numbers that live only in components (the snow trips' size and flurry box, the circus's clearances, the fog) are copied there and checked against their source by `tests/voyageTimeline.test.mjs`, which also checks the projection, the crowding count, the date labels and that every egg `eggs/index.tsx` mounts is charted. A new egg needs a row in `sceneryCatalog.ts`. The sampling and counting are in `voyageTimeline.ts`.
