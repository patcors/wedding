# The Voyage (Garden story, proof of concept)

The Garden's middle chapter becomes a voyage down the river that tells Patrick and Amelia's story, replacing the photo album ("A lifetime of little moments"). The opening (their names) and the ending ("Meet us in the garden") stay. Status: proof of concept on branch `voyage-poc`. Nothing here ships until Amelia says so.

## The story in scenery

Two small sailboats travel downstream as the guest scrolls. Words are minimal: the only running text is the month and year, shown where the photo album used to be. Everything else is told by scenery.

- **Amelia's boat:** a toy sloop (`BOAT_LOOK` in `boatLooks.ts`): a glossy soft teal hull over a white bottom, a little cream cabin, and a white main and jib striped in teal, the jib eased the other way to the main, wing on wing.
- **Patrick's boat:** the same toy sloop in soft yellow. (Other identifying features, not letters, come later.)
- **The opening:** under the title each boat is tied up at its own little jetty on the outer bank of its channel, Amelia's on the left and Patrick's on the right, lanterns still burning from the night. As the guest starts to scroll they let go their lines, ease their sails and pull out into their channels.
  - **The names fly to their boats:** as the guest starts to scroll, "Amelia" glides out of the title to just above her teal boat on the left and "Patrick" to just above his yellow one on the right, so the title's order doesn't leave him on her side. On desktop, where the title is one line, they cross: Amelia's lifts over and Patrick's dips under before either sets off sideways, so they pass well clear of each other; on a phone, where the title is stacked, Patrick's rises as it goes right and Amelia's drops as it goes left. Each shrinks as it goes, landing at about 27 to 46px (3% of the screen's width), legible but small enough that the two never meet over a phone's close boats (if they would, each moves out from between them), and sits a little over its masthead, clear of the 2017 signpost just behind Amelia's mast. Each holds above its boat a moment, following it as it pulls out, then fades there; the "&" and "the journey so far" simply fade where they are as the names lift off. It is all done by .046, before the voyage's date shows at .05 and long before the meeting's stop, and goes by the eased progress the scene shows, so scrolling back brings the names home exactly. Any butterfly perched on the title lets go as the names start to move, and none lands on them until they are home. Before the scene is up, if WebGL fails, or under reduced motion, the title fades as a whole as it always did. (`openingNames.ts`, tested in `tests/openingNames.test.mjs`: `NAME_FLIGHT`'s spans of progress, where each lands and how it bows; `mastheadAt` in `storyBoats.ts`; `NameFlight.tsx` projects the mastheads through the scene's camera each frame and moves the names with CSS variables on the title's panel, never React state, so it works at any screen size.)
- **Before they meet (before July 2017):** the river is split by a small island and thick reeds. Each boat is in its own channel and can't see the other.
- **July 2017, they meet:** the channels rejoin and the boats travel side by side from then on.
- **February 2021 – March 2023 (Amelia in Sydney):** the river widens and the boats drift further apart, still in sight. They part at the nose of a low sandstone rock in the middle of the river, Amelia's on the left and Patrick's on the right, and as February 2021 shows Amelia's sails under a miniature Sydney Harbour Bridge (see "Sydney"). Sydney carries on down the river: across from 2022's pile a miniature Opera House stands on a little sandstone point on her bank. They stay apart through Amelia's two changes of work and come back alongside in March 2023.
- **The flags fly at the sterns:** each boat's university and work flags fly from two small ensign staffs at its stern, one on each quarter, low and in front of the sails as the camera sees them from behind, so the masthead is left bare until the big mast flies their monogram. The work flag flies from the outboard staff (Amelia's to the left, Patrick's to the right) and the university's from the inboard one, towards the other boat, so when both fly they read side by side, each on its own staff. A staff stands only while it has a flag: it is put up just before its first flag goes up and taken down once its last comes down, so the sterns are bare under the title and at the dock.
- **The university's flag:** while they're at university, from the meeting to graduation, both boats fly the University of Wollongong's flag, its shield on paper, from the inboard staff. It is run up as July 2017 shows, flies beside FinoComp's from November 2018, and comes down as August 2020 shows, just before the mortarboards go up.
- **Company flags:** each boat flies a paper flag from its outboard staff with the mark of whoever its sailor works for, and nothing before their internship. Both run up FinoComp's (its orange mark) as November 2018 shows and keep them into Sydney. As August 2022 shows Amelia strikes hers and runs up a plain interim flag for her months tutoring and selling haircare online; as January 2023 shows she runs up HeirWealth's (its purple crown). Patrick keeps FinoComp's until March 2023, so for a while hers is HeirWealth's and his FinoComp's. Each of these is a milestone with time of its own, so the interim flag, and then the two different flags, each fly for most of a screen (about 87svh of scroll each); each change is done as the date settles.
- **August 2024, they start dating:** while the date holds the boats come together and the first two crossbeams go across between them, and butterflies fly out of the trees on Patrick's bank to circle them (see "The dating butterflies"), and his bird has just flown home across the river, as July showed (see "The apartments"). Over the following months the hulls grow long and slim and deck planks are laid one by one between them, until just before their engagement they are one catamaran: two hulls and a shared deck.
- **January 2026, salsa classes:** as the date shows, the catamaran turns once on the spot like a dancer, everything aboard going round with it, and sails on.
- **April 2026, they get engaged:** the two small masts give way to one. Each boat furls its sail and brings its HeirWealth flag down, its stern staff is taken down, and its mast is lowered aft along its hull. While the date holds, one tall mast rises from the middle of the deck; its sail unrolls, the toy sloops' white cloth with each of their two stripes now a band of Amelia's teal under one of Patrick's yellow, and a paper flag with their P&A monogram is run up to its masthead, flying to starboard. On the deck a little man in Patrick's yellow goes down on one knee to a little woman in Amelia's teal and holds up a ring, then stands, and they ride on hand in hand (see "The proposal"). Rose petals trail behind it on the water from the engagement, a few fluttering down from the new sail as it unrolls, and ease off as it enters the bend. The catamaran sails on round the bend and docks under the one sail.
- **The wedding (April 2027):** the river makes an S-bend. Coming round it reveals a massive old tree (the old tree at Jasper's Berry) with a small jetty beneath it. The catamaran docks there.
  - **The finale pulls back (October 2026):** a moment after the catamaran is tied up, the camera rises slowly and draws back upstream over the end of the bend, to a three-quarter view down on the dock: the river coming round, the jetty with the catamaran moored at it (and a guest's own boat by the bank), the old tree with its lights, and the river running on into the dusk. It holds there a couple of seconds and settles back on the dock's view, about eleven seconds in all, a full stop under "the journey so far". The copy, its timing and the dock's view at p 1 are as they were: the flight is timed, not scrolled, and only starts once the boats are moored (`DOCKED`, .97), after the copy has shown (`VOYAGE_END`, .92), so it plays behind the copy and never moves the scroll. The dock chapter has only .03 of progress after mooring (about 70svh, under a second at the story's pace), far too little for a slow rise driven by the scroll, and moving the copy to make room wasn't allowed. "The next chapter" needs nothing new: its last leg ends at p 1, where nothing moves the scroll, so the flight plays out after it as it does after a scroll. It plays once each time they moor (going back into the voyage lets it play again); scrolling back off the mooring brings it down over 1.2 seconds. Reduced motion keeps the still dock, with no flight. A high aerial of the whole river was tried first and left out: from 45 to 70 units up the planted banks end in bare ground a few dozen units from the water and the land stops at 60 either side, with the water plane running on beyond it, and looking back upstream from above the dock showed only canopy, no bend or island; easing the fog back to hide the edges also hid the river. So it rises to 13 units on desktop (7.6 in portrait, which stays under the bend's overhanging branches) and keeps looking downstream, where the river's culling already covers everything it sees; at the top the haze starts 14 units further off (`FINALE.haze`), with the camera's far plane unchanged. Measured on the GPU in October 2026 (headless Chrome on Vulkan, median of 150 frames): desktop 3.3ms a frame at the dock and 3.8 at the top (234 draw calls and 2.49 million triangles, and 271 and 3.19 million, inside the range measured along the voyage); portrait 3.1ms both (241 and 255 calls, 1.61 and 1.94 million).
  - **The guest's own boat (October 2026):** on a Personal link, a little paper boat with a card sail carrying the Party's names (their `greeting`, e.g. "Dean & Kelly") waits beside the jetty when the catamaran comes in, so every guest finishes the story arriving at the wedding too. It drifts down along the jetty's bank from as the wedding's date shows (`GUEST_ARRIVAL`, from p .877) and noses in just upstream of the jetty by .908, before the voyage ends and the copy starts to show; the headland's trees hide that stretch of bank until the camera comes round onto the dock, so the guest first sees it there, settled and rocking gently. It lies between the bank and the catamaran's stern, low and to the right in the dock's picture, below the copy's button on both layouts, its card turned to the dock's camera. The card is a place card: Cormorant Garamond in the Garden's ink on paper with a fine ruled border, set as large as fits on one to three lines, breaking only after a comma or before an "&" ("Kirsten," / "Michael" / "& Kai"), so every greeting in `parties.ts` reads at 12px to the em or more at the dock on a 390 × 844 phone. The boat is full size on a phone and .8 of it on desktop, where the dock's view is nearer. The plain Garden shows no boat: a boat with a blank card would read as a missing name, and the dock's picture there stays exactly as approved. A guest who has opened their Personal link on this device sees their boat on the plain page too (`pa_party`, as the Details page reads it). Reduced motion shows it moored in the dock's still. "Float a boat" boats bump off it once the catamaran is moored, as they do off the catamaran.
- **Dawn to dusk:** the whole journey runs through one clear day, and the light never stands still. The opening is at soft dawn, warm low on the horizon under a pale blue sky; by the time the boats meet it is a bright, light-blue morning, clear blue at midday, a deeper blue through the afternoon, golden round the bend and dusk at the dock. The sun climbs and sinks and swings across the sky the whole way, so the shadows turn as they lengthen, and a light morning haze burns off by midday and gathers again at dusk.

### Adding an egg

The contract is the comment at the top of `eggs/egg.ts`; in short:

- **Register:** add its id to `EggId` and set `egg` on its milestone in `journey.ts`, and map the id to a component in `eggs/index.tsx`. Each egg gets `EggProps`: its `milestone` and the `SlotProps`.
- **Time:** `eggTime(milestone, progress, reduced, settled)` is scroll since the date first showed, in milestone shares (`scrolledSince`): negative before, running through the date's hold. Arrive at 0; linger as long as the story allows, and leave before the dock.
- **Place:** the trees close over the river, so the only open sky is up its corridor, and portrait sees only about 17° either side of the middle. For something in the air, `inView(p, width, { distance, bearing, height, from, follow })` gives a spot relative to the camera (degrees right of the middle; `follow` below 1 lets it lag the camera's turn, as distant things do), `overRiver` keeps it over the water, clear of the trees, and `heightAt(distance, elevation)` turns degrees above the eye line into a height. Place it nearer than it looks and scale it down rather than truly far, where the canopies would hide it. Keep clear of the date label at the top centre; through the bend the only open sky is just left of it (see the balloons). Something on a bank can use a fixed world spot from `riverCenter`/`bankEdge` at `routeAtMonth(month)`.
- **Motion:** hold idle animation while `paused` (set for reduced motion too). Under `reduced` the camera cuts to each milestone's still, where `eggTime` returns `settled`: show the egg at rest there.
- **Budget:** one to three draw calls and well under 1,500 triangles, built once and disposed, no shadows, and `visible = false` outside its window. For comparison the scene draws about 2.7 to 4.05 million triangles a frame on desktop (1440 × 900) and 1.4 to 2.2 million in portrait (390 × 844), in 205 to 305 draw calls (counting the shadow and water-reflection passes; measured at every milestone's stop, every year's turn and the eggs between them in October 2026, after the 2400svh rebalance: the busiest are the meeting and dating's stop, 304 on desktop, and the engagement's petals, 4.05 million triangles).
- **Light:** the sky changes from dawn to dusk, and the haze with it (`scene.fog`'s colour and start). Read colours from `scene.fog` each frame rather than fixing them, as the balloons do for their haze.

## How it is built

All in `src/components/garden/`.

- **`journey.ts`** is the single source of truth, pure and tested (`tests/journey.test.mjs`): `MILESTONES` (`id`, `month`, `description` for screen readers, optional `egg`, `room` and `hold`), `DATES`, when Amelia left FinoComp (`AMELIA_LEFT_FINOCOMP`, August 2022) and when she moved into Patrick's (`AMELIA_MOVED_IN`, March 2026, which nothing is timed by: the birdhouses show it early, `MOVE_SHOWN`), and the chapters over scroll progress p ∈ [0, 1]: arrival `[0, .05)`, voyage `[.05, .92)`, dock `[.92, 1]` (`ARRIVAL_END`, `VOYAGE_END`, `CHAPTERS`, `chapterAt`). The boats are moored from `DOCKED` (.97).
  - `storyMonth(p)`: story time in months since January 2017, piecewise-linear. Scroll is shared out in shares (`ROOM` of progress, about .041, 98svh of scroll). Every year from 2018 to 2023 gets the same scroll, `YEAR` (1.5 shares, 148svh), so the Januaries, and with them the year markers, come evenly however the milestones fall (Amelia's rule). Inside a year each milestone takes its `room` at its date (by default `HOLD`, .35 of a share) and the rest of the year is spread evenly over its twelve months, so a busy year's months rush and a quiet year's roll by. Around them, the run-in (the opening's channels, 1.4 shares, the meeting's 1.4 and the first coast trip's .5) and the run from January 2026 (salsa's .7 and the engagement's .82, each room enough for its scene, the turn and then the rig change, and the trip's 2.98, so its date shows just as the boats enter the bend, before it swings the river, then the wedding's hold) keep lengths of their own: there the months take no scroll and each room runs up to the next date. That makes 21.2 shares. Each date holds for `HOLD` so the label can be read, or for that much of a room longer than a share (the meeting, the trip), or for its own `hold`. `progressAt(month)` is the inverse (when a date first shows), `dateLabel(p)` gives "July 2017" during the voyage and `null` outside it.
  - **2024 and 2025 are longer (Amelia, October 2026).** Until then the rule covered 2024 and 2025 as well, and the run from 2026 had salsa's and the engagement's share each and the trip's 3.4. 2024's three dates took nearly all of its 1.5 shares, so September to December raced by and January 2025, with its stone steps (now the oar), turned almost as soon as dating's date had held; 2025 and 2026 then came 12 units of river apart, and 2027 a long 37 on, round the bend. Amelia asked for 2025 and 2026 to be pushed further towards the end, more evenly spaced, without making the river longer. So `LONG_YEARS` gives 2024 2 shares (196svh) and 2025 1.9 (186svh), and the scroll comes out of the run from 2026: salsa 1 → .7, the engagement 1 → .82 and the trip 3.4 → 2.98. The page, the river and 2017 to 2024 are just as they were. The years now turn (progress, z; the spacing from the year before):

    | Year | Before | Now |

    | 2024 | .555, −93.5 | .555, −93.5 |
    | 2025 | .616, −105.4 (11.9) | .637, −109.3 (15.8) |
    | 2026 | .678, −117.2 (11.9) | .715, −124.4 (15.1) |
    | 2027 | .869, −154.2 (37.0) | .873, −154.9 (30.5) |

    It can go no further: salsa's and the engagement's rooms are about as short as their scenes allow, and the trip's date must still show before the bend swings the river, where the camera turns into it and the headland's trees soon come in front of the far-off Hagia Sophia on a phone. The move had knock-ons, each kept working: from about April 2025 to March 2026 the river bends left and the S-bend ahead draws the camera round after it, putting the right bank at the edge of a phone's picture, so the rocket fired at New Year (it has since moved to October 2026, round the bend) and 2026's arch moved to the left bank (her bird then flew over to his in March, to a second pair of birdhouses on the right bank, until one house on each bank replaced the two pairs); the 2025 snowmen stand nearer the boats; the petals go a little sooner and the Hagia Sophia sinks back into the haze about where it did. `tests/journey.test.mjs` pins the spacing.
  - 2024 is the busiest year, its three dates sharing one year's scroll: May (`room` .43) and June (.58, holding .27 so its room runs on into July) and August (.45, holding .42, nearly all of it) each show for about 46svh, and July for about 20svh; January to April and September to December take about 20svh each run, 5svh a month (they rushed past in a few svh while 2024 was 1.5 shares, when the three dates showed for about 42svh). They showed for about 31svh each when every year was a screen and a third and the page 2110svh; 60svh each, with years still even, would need a page of about 3,400svh; Amelia settled on about 42svh (October 2026), so the page stays at 2400svh. Graduation takes 1.1 shares of 2020 (see "The graduation"), so its date comes early in the year and January to July 2020 roll by quickly. Amelia's interim flag and her HeirWealth flag beside Patrick's FinoComp one each fly for about 87svh (see the company flags).
  - `since(month, p)`: story months since a date (stands still while a date holds). `scrolledSince(month, p)`: scroll since a date in shares, which keeps running; use it for scenery tied to scroll.
  - `routeAt(p)`: where the boats are, as z along the river (x is `riverCenter(z)`). They drift from z 6 to 4 under the title, travel steadily to −164 through the voyage, and glide to the jetty at −172. `routeAtMonth(month)` is where they are on a date. 178 units in all.
  - `stillProgress(p)`: the reduced-motion still for p (the opening, the latest milestone reached, or the dock).
- **The launch jetties:** `launchJetties(width)` (`gardenGeometry.ts`) places one on each outer bank beside `ROUTE.start`, Amelia's a little shorter at the head; the further bank's reaches out further, so both heads are as far from the river's middle. Built in `voyageDock.ts` (`launchJettyGeometry`, its lanterns and `mooringLines`) at a smaller scale than the dock's jetty, Amelia's with a single bollard, a post and her lantern at the head's corner, capped in her teal, Patrick's with two bollards and his lantern back by the bank, capped in his yellow; drawn by `Launch` in `VoyageWorld.tsx`. Each boat lies alongside its jetty's head at p 0, stern to it and bow free (`berth` in `storyBoats.ts`); `castingOff(p)` lets the lines go at p .004 and brings the boats out to the middle of their channels by p .0425, sails easing out as they go (`berthed`), and the lanterns fade. In portrait each jetty is a short landing stage along the bank. The opening looked squashed on a phone (Amelia, October 2026), so there the river opens out under the title (`OPENING_WIDEN`, `openingWiden`: .8 each side upstream of z 7, easing back to its usual width over 6 units downstream, by the island's head), the bend's swings ease off a fifth, and the two jetties stand at the screen's edges, the boats lying out by the bank as on desktop. Planting keeps off the jetties (`voyageClearing`); the first reed bed on the left bank stops just upstream of Amelia's, so it doesn't hide it; guests' boats bump off them (`launchJettyStems`, whose upstream sides slant so a boat glances off into the channel) and taps under them don't launch (`underLaunchJetty`).
- **`gardenGeometry.ts`** shapes the river. `VOYAGE` holds the scenery's positions (z falls downstream; `from` is upstream): the island (z 10 to just before the meeting at about −7.2; in portrait from 7.2, beside the little jetties, as the bend at the start pinches the river above that and its reeds closed the left channel against the bank's; the river opens out there under the title, `openingWiden`), Sydney's widening (from Sydney's date to March 2023's, easing in over 6 units before it), Sydney's rock in the river's middle (`rock`, z −53.6 to −61.3, with the Harbour Bridge across from it at `bridge`, z −60.1; `rockHalfWidth(z, width)` is its footprint), Bennelong Point on the left bank for the Opera House (`point`, z −77.5, −78.3 on a phone; `pointSpot`, and `pointInland` its footprint, which planting keeps off), the S-bend (−135 to −164, swinging the river 12 units to −x), the dock (z −172, jetty on the +x bank), the old tree (z −175.5) and the level lawn for the Hagia Sophia far off past them, back from the left bank (z −202; `lawnSpot`, which `groundHeight` levels, in a field trees keep off). `islandSpan(width)` is where it runs, `islandHalfWidth(z, width)` its footprint and `channelOffset(z, width)` the middle of each channel beside it. `voyageLandmarks(width)` gives the jetty, the catamaran's mooring and the tree in world x/z. `RIVER_REACH` (z 26 to −260) is what's planted. Instances are batched by 20-unit stretch (`riverChunks`) and drawn only within the camera's reach (`useRiverReach`), so the longer river costs about what the old one did.
- **`JourneyProvider.tsx`** eases the scroll into `useJourney().progress` (the old damping, at no more than the story's pace) before any other frame callback; under reduced motion it jumps to `stillProgress`. The page owns that progress (`Pace` in `journeyPace.ts`) and moves it on in its own frames too, so the date and copy show exactly what the scene does. Everything that moves with the story reads it in `useFrame`, so it moves with the camera, not the raw scroll. Slots get `SlotProps`: `paused` (guest pause or reduced motion: hold decorative animation), `reduced`, `mobile` and `width` (1, or `MOBILE_RIVER_WIDTH` on portrait).
- **`cameraRig.ts`** (pure, tested in `tests/cameraRig.test.mjs`) and **`VoyageCamera.tsx`**: the camera rides 12 behind the boats at the old height, looking 34 past them, turning through the bend only as far as keeps the boats within about 10° of the middle of the picture (6° in portrait). Over the start of the dock chapter it settles on `dockView`: over the moored catamaran's stern quarter, so the sail and the monogram's flag read beside the jetty, with the old tree to the right and its lights above the copy (portrait sits higher, with the boats below the copy's button). The camera's far plane now stops just past the fog.
- **The finale** (`cameraRig.ts`, tested in `tests/finale.test.mjs`): `aerialView(width)` is the top of the flight, placed from the mooring like `dockView` (`AERIAL_VIEW`); `finaleLift(t)` is how far up it is `t` seconds after mooring (`FINALE`: a .8s beat, a 4.5s rise, a 2.2s hold and a 4.5s return, smootherstep each way), and `finalePose(p, width, lift)` mixes `cameraPose` towards the aerial by it, so with no lift every view is exactly as before. `VoyageCamera.tsx` keeps the flight's clock (frames count for no more than .05s, so a pause or hidden tab doesn't leap), starts it when the eased progress first reaches `DOCKED`, and writes the lift to the journey clock (`useJourney().lift`), which `GardenSky` reads to start the haze further off.
- **The guest's boat** (`guestBoat.ts`, pure, tested in `tests/guestBoat.test.mjs`; `guestBoatGeometry.ts`; `GuestBoat.tsx`, mounted from `GardenScene.tsx`): `guestBerth(width)` places it by the jetty's bank (`GUEST_BERTH`: its bow `gap` upstream of the jetty, 1 on desktop and 1.1 on a phone, `off` the bank), lying along the bank, with the card's turn to face `dockView`'s camera; `guestBoatPose(p, width)` drifts it down the bank over `GUEST_ARRIVAL`, slowing as it comes; `guestBoatStems(width)` is its footprint for the boats' physics, added with `mooredStems` in `GardenWater.tsx`. `greetingLines` and `fitGreeting` set the names (`CARD_TYPE`); the card's texture (`cardTexture`) is 1024px across, drawn again once Cormorant has loaded, and sampled a mip level sharper (`sharpenFlag`) with a little light of its own so it reads at dusk. The model is the guests' folded paper boat (`foldedPaper`) with a toothpick mast merged into it, its creases and the card: three draw calls, 46 triangles, no shadows (six calls with the water's reflection). The Party comes from `pa_party` in `localStorage`, read with the Details page's `readParty`. Preview it on any Personal link in dev, e.g. `/curry/` (two names) or `/crash/` (three).
- **`GardenScene.tsx`** is wiring only. The rest of the old file moved to `GardenWater.tsx` (water and guest-launched boats), `GardenBankDetails.tsx` and `GardenDrift.tsx` (petals and falling leaves).
- **The boats' rig** (`storyBoats.ts`, `storyBoatGeometry.ts`): `joining(p)` runs from the dating date to the engagement's, so the last plank is down just before April 2026 shows. `catamaran(j)` closes the boats in and sends the two crossbeams across early, while dating's date holds (about .1 to .24 of a share), so dating's stop shows the second going across; the planks follow over the next year and a half. `rigging(p, still)` then times the change of masts by scroll since that date, in shares (`RIG_CHANGE`): the small sails furl and their flags come down (−.3 to −.1), the small masts are lowered aft (−.2 to .04) and the stern staffs are taken down (−.1 to .04), the big mast rises (.04 to .34, while the date holds), its sail unrolls (.3 to .58) and the monogram's flag goes up it (.5 to .8), all before July 2026's balloon. Rose petals trail behind the catamaran from the date (see "The engagement's rose petals"). Under reduced motion each still shows the rig settled: two masts before the engagement, one from it. The big mast (`BIG_RIG`) stands mid-deck and its sail is eased well out to port, away from the jetty, so its face turns to the camera behind and at the dock. Its cloth is the toy sloops' white with their colours joined (`JOINED_STRIPES` in `boatLooks.ts`, drawn by `catamaranSailCloth`): each of the sloops' two stripes is now a band of Amelia's teal directly under one of Patrick's yellow, lower and finer than on the sloops as the sail is so much larger; it unrolls as the plain sail did. Its flag (`LOGO_FLAG`, .52 × .38, larger than the company flags so the monogram reads at the dock on a phone, and short enough to stay clear of the copy beside it on desktop) is `public/logo.png` (ink on transparency) on paper, flying to starboard, clear of the sail.
- **The company flags** (`companyFlags(p, still)` in `storyBoats.ts`): `FLAG_CHANGES` lists each boat's changes in order, each striking the flag before and running the next up the same staff over spans of scroll since its milestone's date, in shares. Every change is a milestone (`internship`, `interim`, `amelia-heirwealth`, `heirwealth`), and each is done within .15 of its share, early in the date's hold, so the milestone's stop (where the scroll cue and arrow keys land, about .17 in) shows the new flag flying and the change plays as the camera glides in. So Amelia's plain interim flag is fully up from p .457 to .493 (about .89 of a share, 87svh of scroll; it used to be .05 of a share), and her HeirWealth flag beside Patrick's FinoComp one from .499 to .535 (about .88 of a share, 86svh, up to March 2023's date; it used to be .13). Change Amelia's leaving month in `AMELIA_LEFT_FINOCOMP` in `journey.ts`: it moves the label and screen-reader date, not the scroll. All are struck as the small sails furl at the engagement. FinoComp's first flags go up from .05 to .15 of November 2018's share, once their staffs are up. The university's flag (`universityFlag(p, still)`, timed in `UNIVERSITY_FLAG`) flies on both boats from the meeting to graduation, from the inboard staff: run up from .05 to .15 of a share as July 2017 shows, so from November 2018 it flies beside FinoComp's, and struck over .08 as August 2020 shows, so it is down before the mortarboards go up and long before graduation's stop. Its flag is the shield itself, `uow-shield.png` (`UNIVERSITY` in `VoyageBoats.tsx`). A reduced-motion still shows each change settled from its month, so the stills show both FinoComp's (November 2018 to February 2021), Amelia's interim flag (August 2022) and her HeirWealth flag beside his FinoComp one (January 2023), then both HeirWealth's (March 2023 to January 2026). The boats stay apart, at four tenths of the way to each bank, through both changes and come back alongside over the last .7 of a share before March 2023. Drawn in `storyBoatGeometry.ts` (`flagGeometry`, `FLAGS`, `companyFlagTexture`): each flag is cut to its logo's shape, named with its mark in `MARKS` in `VoyageBoats.tsx`: FinoComp's a disc of paper .26 across (`finocomp-mark.png`, `#f08805`), HeirWealth's a rounded square of paper .25 across round its crown (`heirwealth-crown.png`, `#783593`), and the university's its shield, .205 × .267. The interim flag's mark is `null` (a plain paper flag, .32 × .23) until Amelia sends the tutoring and haircare logos: name one in `MARKS.interim`, or for two, add a company to `Company` and a change to Amelia's list in `FLAG_CHANGES`.
- **The ensign staffs** (`ensignStaffs(p, still)` and `ENSIGN_STAFFS` in `storyBoats.ts`; `ENSIGN`, `ensignStaff` and `ensignStaffGeometry` in `storyBoatGeometry.ts`): two small staffs at each stern, .46 tall from just above the floor (`ENSIGN`), on the dinghy's quarters at six tenths of the stern's half-beam, and following the stern aft to the catamaran hull's centreline as the hulls grow long (`quarter` on each hull shape). Each flag hangs from the top of its staff and runs .17 up and down it. The outboard staff carries the company's flags and the inboard one the university's, so with the flags .32 long the two inboard flags leave water between them when the boats are alongside. A staff rises out of its foot over .05 of a share as its first flag's date shows (the inboard one as July 2017 shows, the outboard one as November 2018 shows), and its flag goes up from .05 to .15, so each milestone's stop still shows it flying. All four staffs are one instanced draw call (`staffs` in `VoyageBoats.tsx`, 128 triangles, no shadows), placed each frame in their boat's frame; the flags are drawn as before, one draw call each while up. Checked in the browser at 1440 × 900 and 390 × 844 at the meeting, the internship, July 2019, graduation, August 2022, January and March 2023, salsa, the engagement and the dock: both flags read side by side through 2019 on both sizes, the flags fly in front of the sails, and no sail or hull hides them.
- The story boats are kinematic: their positions are a function of progress (`storyBoats.ts`). They don't use `boatPhysics.ts`, which stays for the guest-launched "Float a boat" boats. Those keep to the channels round the island and Sydney's rock (`islandHalfWidth` and `rockHalfWidth` are the physics' `island` shore, the rock's held closer, `islandMargin`), and bump off the little jetties at the start, the Harbour Bridge's pylons, Bennelong Point and the jetty at the dock (`launchJettyStems`, `bridgeStems`, `pointStems`, `jettyStems`) and, once it is tied up, the moored catamaran (`mooredStems`). In portrait the channels at the island's head are narrower than a paper boat, which can catch there; "Float a boat" launches beside the island past its head.

Keyframes sit on milestones, at most a few milestones' scroll apart, and the sky moves in straight lines between them, so it never settles:

  The sun's azimuth swings about 150° from dawn to dusk while its elevation climbs to midday and falls. `haze` sets the fog's start between a clear day's (38, or 22 in portrait) and the old mist's (16, or 8); the far end is fixed. Each key's `waterLight` multiplies the river's own colour (a `WaterStyle`), so any water brightens in the morning, warms in the golden light and dims at dusk, and `glint` is the sun's sparkle on it. `lampsAt(p)` brings the festoons and the jetty's lantern up through the second half of the dusk, so they glow as the old tree comes round the bend.
- **Seasons:** the avenue looked the same from 2017 to 2027, so the years passed on the date but not in the world. Now the year turns in the canopy, the grass and the light, through the year as it falls in New South Wales, with nothing added to the scene. `seasons.ts` (pure, tested in `tests/seasons.test.mjs`) gives each season's amounts by story time (`storyMonth`), and `GardenSeasons.tsx` writes them each frame into one set of uniforms (`SEASON_UNIFORMS`) that the leaves, ground and plants share, injected with `onBeforeCompile`: no extra draw calls, no new textures, and no shader compiles while scrolling (only uniforms change). Each key holds over its months (a milestone's date holds on the 1st of its month, so July is 6 to 7) and the year eases from one to the next between them:

  | Months | Key | Leaves | Grass and plants | Light | Petals |
  | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
  | 3 to 4.7 (April to mid-May) | `AUTUMN` | the deciduous trees gold, amber or russet card by card, about a seventh of their leaves already down | towards straw-gold (`dry`) | sun a touch warmer | .6 of them |
  | 6 to 7.4 (July to mid-August) | `WINTER` | two thirds of the deciduous trees' leaves down, so their branches show, the rest dull brown; every tree a little greyer (`bare`, `grey`) | greyer, cooler green | sun 8% weaker and a little cooler | .35 |
  | 8.5 to 10.1 (mid-September to October) | `SPRING` | fresh, yellower green, and a pink blossom wash on a third of the deciduous trees (`fresh`, `blossom`) | fresher green | fill light 2% brighter | all, paler like blossom |
  | 11.3 to 13.7 (mid-December to mid-February) | `SUMMER` | a little richer (`lush`) | a little richer | sun 2% stronger | all |

  About two trees in five are deciduous, chosen by a hash of where each stands (`seasonalLeaves`); the rest stay evergreen, with only the winter grey and summer richness. The old tree at the dock (`seasonEvergreen`) keeps its leaves whatever the season, as the venue's tree's kind isn't the scene's to say. The leaves' fall is a discard of whole leaf cards in the colour and the shadow (depth) material alike, so the shadows thin with the canopy. The grass's wash (`seasonGround`) is on the natural ground's turf, not its mud, and on every plant kind (island planting too); the light's share (`seasonLight`) multiplies the sky's sun and fill light in `GardenSky.tsx` by a few percent at most, so it never fights the day. The falling leaves turn gold, amber and russet in autumn and brown in winter (`leafColour`), and the drifting petals pale in spring and thin through autumn and winter (`petalColour`, `petalShown`). The colours all scale the textures' own brightness, so both texture sets (Light and Original) and both tree palettes work alike. So the snowman's flurry (July 2019) and the snowmen holding hands (August 2025) stand in winter, May 2024's apartments and the engagement in autumn, February 2021's Harbour Bridge in summer, and November 2018's internship in late spring; from the trip (July 2026) the last stretch runs winter, spring, summer and into autumn at the dock, in the dusk.
  - **Months pass unevenly in scroll**, so the season read straight from the month would flash: 2023's April to December go by in about 12svh. So `seasonAt(p)` takes the time of year averaged over the scroll round p, as an angle round the year, weighted by a triangle `SMOOTHING` (.3) shares either side (`yearAround`). Story time is sampled once on a fine grid of progress and is straight between points, so the average is worked out exactly on each piece and moves smoothly with p. Where months roll by steadily (about 148svh a year) it follows them; where they rush, the angles cancel and the wash fades towards none rather than flickering through the seasons; the long last stretch (about 32svh a month) turns slowly. The test checks it never jumps, and that the page has nine to eleven autumns, not flashes. Under reduced motion each still shows its own month's season exactly (`seasonAt(p, false)`).
  - **Review panel (dev only):** *Seasons* (On, the default, and what guests see: `SEASONS`; or Off, the garden as before, all year) switches at once, without a reload, and is remembered in `localStorage` (`pa_dev_seasons`). The panel is a button wider; at 1,440px it still wraps onto two rows, and on a phone it fits the grid as before.
- **Review panel (dev only):** *Sky* switches between the clear day and the Garden's old misty day (`MIST_KEYS`, unchanged, for comparison); it reloads, so Safari's bars and the cover open in that sky's colours. *Water* picks the river's colour from `WATERS`: deep blue (Patrick's clear-day water, the default), clear blue, teal, sage mist (the old river) and a dark mirror. Both are remembered in `localStorage` (`pa_dev_sky`, `pa_dev_water`). *Boats* tries four looks for the dinghies (`DINGHY_LOOKS` in `boatLooks.ts`): the toy sloop (the default), the original wooden sailboat, a dark lugger with a wave-banded lug sail and a spritsail skiff. *Catamaran* tries four for what they become (`CATAMARAN_LOOKS`): the original (the default), whose hulls keep the dinghies' paint, under a wooden deck and a white sail striped in their teal and yellow, and three cruisers with white or ivory hulls, a cabin and a jib, one with P&A over 27 on its main. Guests see `BOAT_LOOK`. Both are remembered (`pa_dev_dinghy`, `pa_dev_catamaran`). On one line the panel is about 1,660px wide; under 1,700px it wraps onto two rows, and on portrait phones it is a four-row grid; the scroll cue and the test buttons are lifted above it.
- **Inspect and notes (dev only, `inspect/`):** a way to point at things in the scene instead of describing them ("the Sydney house", "the duck near that area"). *Inspect* in the Review panel turns it on (remembered, `pa_dev_inspect`). Hovering anything on desktop, or tapping it on a phone, shows a small label: a human name ("Harbour Bridge", "Amelia's boat", "pie-chart flower bed", "2021 year marker", "tree (leaves)", "the old tree", "flag staff, Patrick's side"), the story moment it belongs to (a milestone's id and day, or when it shows), the file it is made in, and where it is: its world position, which bank (Amelia's left or Patrick's right, or in the river), the progress p and the date on screen. ## Tools

### Screenshots

`corepack pnpm shots` (`scripts/voyage-shots.mjs`, its pure parts in `scripts/voyage-shots-lib.mjs`, tested in `tests/voyageShots.test.mjs`) photographs the voyage at every milestone stop in headless Chrome, with a contact sheet and a visual diff, so nobody needs a throwaway screenshot script. `--help` lists every option.

- **What it shoots:** the opening, each milestone's stop and the dock: `STOPS` in `journey.ts`, where the arrow keys and the scroll cue land, halfway through each date's hold. Desktop 1440 × 900 and phone 390 × 844, the phone emulated as one (mobile layout, touch, no hover). `--only meet,engaged,dock` picks stops by id, `--at .3,.55` adds any scroll progress (on its own, only those), and `--viewports` takes `desktop`, `phone`, `phone-small` (320 × 568), `phone-mid` (375 × 667), `phone-landscape` (844 × 390), `WxH` or `WxH-touch`. The stops always come from the checkout running the command, whichever server it shoots.
- **Stills by default.** It emulates reduced motion, so each stop is the scene's still for that milestone, with no butterflies or animation, and waits for downloads, fonts and the page's fades (the cover, the scroll cue) before each shot. Two runs of the same code match to within 1 level of 255 in a few dozen pixels. `--live` scrolls as a guest does instead, waits as long as the story's pace takes to get there (`paceToward` in `journeyPace.ts`) and 2.5s more for the scene to settle; it is slower and never quite the same twice.
- **Where:** `--serve` (the default) starts this checkout's dev server on a free port from 4800 (or `--port`), never 4321, 4341 or 4342, and stops it afterwards. `--url http://127.0.0.1:4351` shoots a server already running. The dev Review panel is tucked away unless `--review`. Astro 7 sends `astro dev` to the background when it detects an AI agent, so `--serve` starts it with `ASTRO_DEV_BACKGROUND=1` and `--ignore-lock` to keep it a child with its own PID (and alongside any `pnpm dev` of the same checkout).
- **Output:** `.shots/<branch>-<date>-<time>/` in the checkout (git-ignored, and not watched by the dev server; `--out` to choose): a PNG per stop and viewport (`desktop/05-graduation.png`), a contact sheet per viewport with the date and stop id under each frame (`contact-phone.png`), `manifest.json`, and `index.html`, which flips through the frames: ← → step stops, ↑ ↓ change viewport, B shows after, before and the difference.
- **Comparing:** `--compare <dir>` diffs against an earlier run. `--compare-url http://127.0.0.1:4342` shoots the same stops from another server as "before" (into `before/`), so a branch can be checked against a review server in one go. Each frame gets a before | after | difference image in `compare/`, the changed pixels in magenta over a faded copy, and the summary, printed, in `compare/summary.txt` and at the top of the index, lists the frames that changed, most first. A pixel counts as changed when a colour channel moves more than 8 levels, and a frame when more than .01% of its pixels do (`THRESHOLDS`); live, 48 levels and 2.5%, since there the water's shimmer and the trees' sway move 1 to 4% of pixels by more than 24 levels between two runs of the same code. `--threshold` and `--min-changed` override them. If the dev server swaps in changed code part way through (an edit while it runs), it notices and shoots that viewport again.
- **Clean-up:** Chrome gets a profile of its own in the system's temp folder, deleted afterwards. Chrome and the dev server each start in a process group of their own and are stopped by their PIDs, whether the run finishes, fails or is stopped with Ctrl-C. `--timeout` (90s) bounds the wait for the scene to be ready. Chrome is `/usr/bin/chromium` or the usual macOS places, or `--chrome` (or `CHROME=`); on Linux it draws with the GPU through Vulkan (SwiftShader takes minutes a frame), and `CHROME_FLAGS` adds flags.
- **Time:** a full run (18 stops, desktop and phone) takes about 35s against a running server and 50s with `--serve`, whose first load compiles the page, plus about 6s to compare; `--compare-url` shoots twice, about 70s in all. Other work on the GPU can slow it. `--live` takes about four and a half minutes for every stop on both, most of it the story's own pace from stop to stop, so it is worth narrowing with `--only`.
- One thing to know: in October 2026, on the first run after a source edit, about one run in four had a single frame that differed from the same code's: trees, grass and sails at another point in their sway, or the whole view shifted a little. It looks like the scene's wind clocks and boat physics, which step with rendered frames, catching a frame or two of the slower first load after the change; in some twenty runs with no fresh edit it never happened. Run again before trusting a lone change you didn't expect.

### Timeline

**Timeline (dev only).** `/dev/timeline` charts the whole voyage on one page, so spacing and crowding can be directed at a glance. Its x axis is scroll progress p from 0 to 1, with the chapters, the sky's keyframes as a colour strip (`skyCycle.ts`), each January and each milestone (a diamond where its date first shows, a ring at its stop). Under it every scenery piece has a row: the eggs, ornaments, carvings, year markers, what happens on the boats (the flags, the joining, the salsa turn, the change of masts, the proposal, the petals, the butterflies) and the river's landforms. A solid bar is when the piece is really in the picture and competing for attention, coloured by bank (Amelia's left teal, Patrick's right yellow, the river or both grey); a pale bar is in the picture but not counted (a flag simply flying, the monogram, the landforms); an outline is fading in or out, or mostly lost in the fog; a thin line is drawn but out of the picture. "In the picture" is worked out, not read off the timings: p is sampled every .0005, the camera is put where `cameraRig.ts` puts it on desktop (1440 × 900) and on a phone (390 × 844), each piece's bounding sphere (from its own geometry where it can be built in Node) is projected into the frustum, and it counts where at least half the sphere is in the frame, it is at least two pixels across, the fog (GardenSky's) hides less than half of it and its own show/hide function has it at least half faded in. Trees and the headland aren't modelled, so a piece hidden in leaves still counts. Real-time pieces (tosses, flights, the salsa turn, the rocket) are drawn where they come to rest and counted from their cue to their own `late`. The lane at the foot counts the competing pieces in view at each p and shades where more than two are (`CROWDED`). Hovering a row shows its name, its windows in dates and p, and its file; hovering the lane lists what is in view; clicking anywhere offers the Garden at that moment, `/?at=<p>` (the `?at=` parameter comes from the `voyage-c-inspect` branch); the toggle switches desktop and phone, and Zoom stretches the axis. The same numbers as text, for checking crowding without a browser: `node scripts/voyage-timeline.mjs` lists the busiest moments and the crowded stretches on both layouts (`--layout phone`, `--pieces` for every piece's windows, `--at 0.62` for what is in view at a moment, `--json`). The pieces come from `sceneryCatalog.ts`, built from the scene's own exported spots, show/hide functions and geometry; the few numbers that live only in components (the snow trips' size and flurry box, the circus's clearances, the fog) are copied there and checked against their source by `tests/voyageTimeline.test.mjs`, which also checks the projection, the crowding count, the date labels and that every egg `eggs/index.tsx` mounts is charted. A new egg needs a row in `sceneryCatalog.ts`. The sampling and counting are in `voyageTimeline.ts`.
